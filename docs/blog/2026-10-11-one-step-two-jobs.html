No. 7: One step, two jobs

2026-10-11Pawn single-step key

Ten units and a quiet pawn key. The pawn has two things to do, and only one square lets it do both.

Solution
Key
1.c3! (2.Qd4#)
1...Se6 2.fxe6#
1...Sxf5 2.Rxf5#
1...Se4 2.Qxe4#
Comment

What it is

The key is a pawn single step with two jobs. On c3 the pawn protects d4, so the threat 2.Qd4# works. Leaving c2, it opens the bishop's diagonal b1-f5, which two mates use: 1...Se4 2.Qxe4# and 1...Sxf5 2.Rxf5# (in the set play 1...Sxf5 has no mate). The knight g7 defends twice by guarding d4, and each time the half-battery Rh5/Pf5 answers.

Flaws

  • Random moves of the knight f2 (Sg4, Sh3, Sh1, Sd1) unguard e4, so 2.Qe4# mates as well as the threat. Duals after unthematic moves.
  • No tries. The planned 1.c4? (the double step) never existed: the queen stands on c4.
  • The white king h8 takes no part; it only stands off the knights' checks.
  • Mixed defences: two guard d4, one blocks e4. No common motive (the critique's 'second-layer unity missing').

Pluses

  • A quiet key with two reasons.
  • Three different mates, one of them (Rxf5) added by the key.
  • 7+3, and the trim tool cannot remove any unit.

How it was made

Idea on paper, two kernels, one defender search, one cleaning step: about 25 minutes, 9 solves, 1 search. The stages with their diagrams are below.

Lesson

Test the planned try at the kernel stage, not at judging: the queen's post chosen for the box killed the double-step try, and nobody noticed for five stages.

What changed in the kit

Posts carry a 'development' section: every stage of the composing process with its diagram, FEN, change, solver verdict and decision, and the rejected search hits side by side (tools/blog.py dev_html; tested in tests/test_blog.py).

How it was made, stage by stage
  1. Stage 0: idea

    Why would a pawn step be the only key?

    Change
    Three reasons: open a line for a piece behind it, give a flight, guard a square the threat needs. Choice: Bb1 behind Pc2, black king e5; 1.c3 opens b1-f5 and guards d4. Planned threat 2.Qe4#, planned try 1.c4? (the double step guards d5, not d4).
  2. Stage 1: kernel 1

    Box the king, no Black men

    4N3/8/8/4kP2/2Q3P1/8/2P5/1B5K
    Change
    Se8 guards d6, f6; Pf5 guards e6; Pg4 protects f5; Qc4 holds d4, e4, f4, d5, e6.
    Solver
    No solution: every White move stalemates or frees a square.
    Decision
    Black needs a unit whose moves defend.
  3. Stage 2: kernel 2

    Add a black knight f2

    4N3/8/8/4kP2/2Q3P1/8/2P2n2/1B5K
    Change
    +bSf2: Sd3 would cut b1-e4, Se4 blocks, Sxg4 unguards f5.
    Solver
    Illegal: the knight checks Kh1.
    Decision
    Move the White king out of reach.
  4. Stage 3: kernel 2b

    King to h8: the kernel is sound

    4N2K/8/8/4kP2/2Q3P1/8/2P2n2/1B6
    Change
    Kh1 to h8.
    Solver
    1.c3! unique. Threat 2.Qd4#; 1...Se4 2.Qxe4#.
    Decision
    The planned threat was wrong (Sf2 guards e4); the solver's is better: the pawn guards d4 for the threat, the opened line serves the mate after Se4. One defence only: add defenders.
