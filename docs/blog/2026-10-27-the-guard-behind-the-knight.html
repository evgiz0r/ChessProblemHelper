No. 21: The guard behind the knight

2026-10-27Grimshaw

The rook on d8 guards the d-file only as far as the knight on d4. Black's rook and bishop can both reach d6. Every knight move threatens something; only one square makes the threat stick. Which, and why do the others fail?

Solution

1...Bd6 a 2.Qc6# A

1...Bd6 a 2.Qc6# A

Key
1.Sf3! (2.Sd2#)
1...Bd6 a 2.Qc6# A
1...Rd6 b 2.Qxe5# B
Comment

What it is

A Grimshaw on d6 that only the key turns into defences. The knight d4 masks the rook d8's guard of d3, d4 and d5; every knight move threatens a knight check whose flights the rook then closes. The black rook and bishop parry by interfering on d6, cutting the d-file, and each interference also cuts the other piece's guard beyond d6: 1...Rd6 cuts the bishop b8 from e5 (2.Qxe5#, the queen down the e-file), 1...Bd6 cuts the rook h6 from c6 (2.Qc6#, the queen on the diagonal e8-c6). The knight's square is chosen by those mates: only from f3 does it both protect e5 and still threaten a check. The random knight moves fail to 1...Rd6!, the correction 1.Se6? (which shuts the rook out of d6) fails to 1...Rxe6!, and 1.Bxh6?, which takes the rook, fails to 1...Bd6!.

Flaws

  • The random tries carry two threats each (1.Sb3? 2.Sd2#/Sxc5#, 1.Se2? 2.Sg3#/Sc3#), and so does 1.Bxh6? (2.Qg6#/Qc6#).
  • The try for the bishop's variation, 1.Bxh6?, captures one of the two Grimshaw pieces; no knight move is refuted by 1...Bd6 alone. Of the knight's squares only f3 and c6 protect e5, and on c6 the knight has no check to threaten (try table), so the pair cannot be two knight moves in this frame.
  • The key knight stands attacked by the pawn c5 (1...cxd4 in the diagram): the key looks like an escape. The pawn also gives 1.Se6? its refutation: without it 1.Se6 solves as well.
  • 2.Qxe5# captures a pawn (minor, E. Bourd 13 Oct 2026). The pawn e5, pinned by the queen, is what keeps the king off e5 in the threat.
  • Cook-stoppers: the knight f2 guards g4 (without it both Grimshaw moves are also answered by 2.Qg4#, and 1.Qd7 solves); the pawn c5 (above).
  • Duals after 11 moves that do not defend (1...Ba7 2.Qxe5#/Sd2#; the rook's moves along the h-file 2.Qg6#/Qc6#/Sd2#).
  • No set mates for 1...Bd6 and 1...Rd6, and no link between the two thematic mates beyond the queen and the white king.

Pluses

  • The second layer reaches both thematic variations, as issue #21 asked: 1...Rd6 refutes the random knight moves, 1...Bd6 refutes 1.Bxh6?.
  • Dombrovskis effect: 1.Bxh6? threatens 2.Qc6# among others, 1...Bd6! refutes it, and after the key the same 1...Bd6 allows 2.Qc6#.
  • A white correction: the knight's random moves give up nothing the threat needs but leave e5 unprotected (1...Rd6!); the correction 1.Se6? shuts the black rook out of d6 and can be taken (1...Rxe6!); 1.Sf3! protects e5 and holds d4 for the other mate.
  • Quiet key, no capture, no check, no flight in the diagram. The queen mates from e8 on two lines. The white king is the only guard of d3 in both thematic mates. 13 units.

How it was made

Theme from the rotation: Grimshaw (last used on 11 October, daily No. 6, whose one try reached one variation only). Study: Ganapathi 2003 (tries by the key knight, each refuted by one Grimshaw move), Wessels 2000 (Grimshaw with Dombrovskis), Bourd 2004 (Zagoruiko 4x2 by queen posts), Selb 1953, Elbaz 2000. First plan, set Grimshaw mates changed by a quiet queen key: a scratch enumerator of the geometry (threat through the crossing, interposition cuts, two queen posts) returned 204 frames for central kings, every one his bench frame of 15 October or his 2004 problem reflected or rotated; dropped. Second plan, the white key knight standing on the crossing e7 in No. 6's frame: proved impossible by hand, because 2.Qd6#'s guards are a subset of 2.Sd7#'s (Sd7 blocks the queen's file), so no try can fail to 1...Re7 alone; eight evolve jobs had found nothing there. Kit change: evolve DEFTRIES and a fix for PATTERN=Grimshaw (it never matched). Third frame, Ke4 with the d-file guard unmasked by the knight d4: unmask_search with the two mates and the tries fixed (16 jobs) gave sound post-keys with three tries refuted by 1...Rd6 and none by 1...Bd6. Reasoned from the try tables: e5's protection must depend on the knight's square, and only f3 and c6 give it; a masked battery (Ra4 behind Bc4) gave 1.Sc6? Bd6! but ten discharge threats. The search's sound 15-unit setting (pawn c6) became 15 units with c6 free (x33), then the king c2 freed the pawn b3 and the bishop f1 went. About 35 hand solves, 24 search jobs, 7 frames.

Attempts that failed

Fifteen units, random tries with one threat each
1b1RQ3/8/7r/2p1p1B1/3Nk3/1p2N3/5nP1/1K3B2

Same key and mates; the tries 1.Sb5? (2.Sc3#) and 1.Sf5? (2.Sg3#) Rd6! each have a single threat, and the bishop f1 covers d3 in both thematic mates. The black pawn b3 only shields the king b1 from 1...Rb6+. Gate: PASS.

Sound, but a single phase (flight-giving key)
1b1R1N1Q/8/r7/7p/R1BNk3/4P1p1/6K1/6B1

1.Sf3! (2.Bd3#) Bd6 2.Qh7#, Rd6 2.Qe5#, and the flight 1...Kf5 is answered by the threat. The knight tries fail to two or more of 1...Rf6 (the rook beyond d6 blocks the queen's diagonal to e5), 1...Kf5, 1...Bd6 and 1...Rd6; 1.Sc6? fails to the king alone. No thematic try.

The masked battery draft
1b1RQ3/8/7r/4p1B1/R1BNk1P1/8/K5P1/8

Ra4 behind Bc4 behind the knight d4: 1.Sc6? Bd6! appears, because the knight on c6 protects e5 and stands on the mating square of 2.Qc6#. But every bishop move is a discovered mate, so each knight move threatens up to eleven mates, and 1.Qb5 and 1.Se6 also solve.

Lesson

Before searching for a try pair against two thematic mates, list what each mate needs from the key piece. A try refuted by defence A alone exists only if mate A needs something mate B does not: in No. 6's frame every guard of 2.Qd6# is also a guard 2.Sd7# needs, so the search there could never succeed; here 2.Qxe5# needs e5 protected and 2.Qc6# needs d4, which every knight square gives.

What changed in the kit

evolve: DEFTRIES=1 (or 2 for tries by the key piece only) rewards a quiet try refuted by each thematic defence alone, answered after the key, and a HIT needs one per DEF: the second layer that reaches every thematic variation (mentor issue #21). And PATTERN=Grimshaw now matches: the solver names a Grimshaw's pair in one string ('Bd6/Rd6 on d6'), which never equalled a DEF before, so every Grimshaw search scored no pattern. Tests in tests/test_evolve.py.
