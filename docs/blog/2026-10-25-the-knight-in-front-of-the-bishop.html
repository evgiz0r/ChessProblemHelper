No. 20: The knight in front of the bishop

2026-10-25Self-blocks

The black knight on f7 stands in front of its own bishop. Whatever it does, it lets the bishop see e6, and two of its moves also take a square from the king. Which white units are waiting for that?

Solution
Set play
1...Sd6 a 2.Sc7# A
1...Se5 b 2.Se7# B
Try
1.Bc7? (2.Se7#)
1...Bc5 2.Sc3#
but 1...Sd6! a
Key
1.Qc8! (2.Qe6#)
1...S~ (Sh8, Sh6, Sg5) 2.Qd7#
1...Sxd8 2.Qd7#/Qxd8#
1...Sd6 a 2.Sc7# A
1...Se5 b 2.Se7# B
Comment

What it is

Two self-blocks by one black knight, each answered by the White knight that was the only guard of that square: Sb5 holds d6 and leaves for c7 when Black blocks d6, Sc6 holds e5 and leaves for e7 when Black blocks e5. The key 1.Qc8! threatens 2.Qe6# (protected by the pawn f5). Every move of the knight f7 opens the line g8-e6 for its own bishop and so stops the threat; the random moves lose to 2.Qd7#, and the two self-blocks, which also stop 2.Qd7 (Sd6 interposes, Se5 guards d7), err by blocking the king. The try 1.Bc7? replaces the d6 guard with a bishop (c7-d6-e5) and threatens 2.Se7#; 1...Sd6! refutes it twice: it cuts the bishop's diagonal to e5, and c7, the square of its own mate, is occupied.

Flaws

  • The thematic mates are set and the key keeps them: the key only adds the threat and the random moves' mate. The second phase is one try, refuted by one of the two self-blocks; no try is refuted by 1...Se5 (searched, not found).
  • 1...Sxd8 2.Qd7#/Qxd8#: a dual after a random knight move.
  • The bishop d8 takes part in no mate: it plays the try 1.Bc7? and stops 2.Qd8# as a dual after 1...Sh8/Sh6/Sg5.
  • Cook-stoppers: the black bishop g1 (stops 1.Qa7/1.Qb6, refutes 1.Bf6? and 1.Qxa5? with 1...Bc5), the pawn a5 (shuts the a-file, 1.Qa2# otherwise, and guards b4 against 2.Sb4 after 1...Se5). The king h1 is passive.
  • The knight f7's other four moves (Sh8, Sh6, Sg5, Sxd8) stop the threat only because the bishop's line opens, and 2.Qd7# answers them all: random moves, not defences (E. Bourd, 26 Oct 2026).

Pluses

  • Unity: one black knight carries the whole defence. Its departure from f7 is the defence (line opening for Bg8), its arrival squares are the errors (self-blocks), and each error is punished by the White knight that guarded that square.
  • A thematic try refuted by a thematic defence with two effects (interference on c7-e5 and occupation of the mating square's neighbour line: c7 is taken by the try piece).
  • Quiet key, no capture, no check; the queen leaves the 6th rank for the c8-e6 diagonal, the only line that reaches e6 without guarding d6 or e5 (either would spoil a released guard).
  • Connected mates: the two White knights hold d4 for each other (Sc6 while Sb5 mates on c7, Sb5 while Sc6 mates on e7); the pawn f5 holds e6 in every mate.

How it was made

Theme from the rotation: self-blocks (last used on 10 October). Bare-core searches failed first: evolve with SELFBLOCK=2 from four cores (rook and bishop self-blocking next to Kd5/Ke5/Kd4) gave only checking keys; postsearch with a fixed threat 2.Qc6# and three self-blocks guarding c6 (a black knight to d4/e5, a rook to d6) and freethreat with a rank-guard scheme found no mate for any knight block in about 750,000 positions. The way out was E. Bourd's principle from his 7 October sketch: a self-block mate exists when the only guard of the blocked square is the unit that mates. Built by hand, one solve per change: Sb5 (d6) and Sc6 (e5) as the only guards; the black knight on f7 masking Bg8 from e6 so that every knight move defends; the e6 protector had to be a pawn (f5), because a bishop or the king either mated on e6 as well (double threat) or guarded d6 (Ke7 spoils 2.Sc7). Three White knights crept in once and the solver flagged it. keyfind --patch gave 1.Qa6-c8! with a black bishop as the only unit added; evolve around that position (SELFBLOCK=2, second layer required) found the bishop d8 with the try 1.Bc7? Sd6! and the dual stop; trimming removed the rest. On the way, the gate passed a draft as two-phased only because of the king-refuted try 1.Scd4? Ke5!: fixed (see the improvement). About 40 hand solves, 28 search jobs.

Lesson

Name the mate before the defence: a self-block has a mate only when the square it blocks was guarded by exactly one White unit, and that unit can mate from elsewhere. Every blind self-block search today failed for want of this; the hand construction around two sole guards worked in about 20 solves.
