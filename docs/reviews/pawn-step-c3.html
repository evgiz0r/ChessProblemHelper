A pawn single step as key: 1.c3!

Task from E. Bourd: compose a simple two-mover whose key is a white pawn single step. From idea to gate in about 25 minutes, nine solver runs and one defender search (44 s). Every position below was solved; nothing in this review is hand analysis.

Final position · 7+3 · mate in 2

4N2K/6n1/8/4kP1R/2Q5/8/2P2n2/1B6 w - - 0 1

  • 1.c3! (threat 2.Qd4#)
  • 1…Se6 2.fxe6#
  • 1…Sxf5 2.Rxf5#
  • 1…Se4 2.Qxe4#

The step has two jobs. The pawn on c3 protects d4, so the threat Qd4# works. Leaving c2, it opens the diagonal b1–f5 for two mates: Qxe4 and Rxf5. In the set play 1…Sxf5 has no mate. The knight g7 defends twice by guarding d4, and each time the half-battery Rh5/Pf5 answers.

Open the Composing Bench

Flaws, then pluses

  • Random moves of Sf2 (Sg4, Sh3, Sh1, Sd1) unguard e4, so Qe4# mates as well as the threat. Duals after unthematic moves.
  • No tries. The planned 1.c4? never existed: the queen stands on c4.
  • wKh8 takes no part; it only stands off the knights' checks.
  • Mixed defences: two guard d4, one blocks e4. No common motive.
  • Quiet key with two reasons; three different mates; 10 units, nothing removable (trim).
  • One mate (Rxf5) is added by the key.

How it was made, stage by stage

Stage 0 · idea on paper

Why would a pawn step be the only key?

Three possible reasons: it opens a line for a piece behind it, it gives a flight, or it guards a square the threat needs. Choice: a bishop b1 behind a pawn c2, black king e5. 1.c3 opens b1–f5 and guards d4. Planned threat 2.Qe4#, planned try 1.c4? (the double step guards d5 instead of d4).

Solver
1.c3! unique. Threat 2.Qd4#; 1…Se4 2.Qxe4#.
Decision
The planned threat Qe4# was wrong (Sf2 guards e4). The solver's threat is better: the pawn guards d4 for the threat and the opened line serves the mate after Se4. One defence only, so add defenders.

What the process shows

  • The hand plan was wrong twice: the threat (Qe4 → Qd4) and the try (1.c4? impossible with the queen on c4). The second was only caught at judging time; the try should have been tested at stage 1.
  • The search's top hits looked richer (more defences) but mated on one square twice. The ranking needs a "distinct mating squares" criterion.
  • Critique caught the set-only unit at once; removing it also gave the key a second use of the opened line.

Open questions

  • Are the duals after Sf2's random moves acceptable in a simple problem?
  • Is the key's double job enough content?
  • Hunt the 1.c4? try with the queen elsewhere on the 4th rank, so the double step closes her road to d4?
