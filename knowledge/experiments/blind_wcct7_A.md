> Section A: Twomovers
> Theme: Single-phase 2-movers with a blend of at least three different thematic groups of two or more variations, i.e. at least 6 variations altogether. Two of the thematic groups may show the same theme but must be clearly recognizable as separate systems. There should be different mates in all thematic variations, and none of the thematic variations should appear in more than one thematic group.

## Disagreements and what they teach (out of sample: no checklist line came from this field)
1. **A blend is judged by its weakest group.** Nearly every remark names the weakest system: "last system inferior and familiar"
   (1st), "third system of three WK mates much weaker" (8th, our 1st), "claimed fourth pair disparate and weak", "artificially added
   variations" (19th, our 3rd, with plugs called "pure weasels"), "first three groups one system" (21st, our 2nd). Our judges added
   groups up; the countries marked down for the weakest one. A group that is the same mechanism as another, or a pair of king
   captures, lowers a blend instead of raising it.
2. **The key counts double in a single-phase theme** (2nd place, Stolev, our 12th): "best and most thematic key of the tourney -
   double sacrifice, unpin, flight-giving, change of 4 mates". Our judge called its groups a mixed set and did not weigh the key.
3. **Lazy white pieces and plugs cost marks even at the top** (2nd: "marred by lazy WBb7 and plugs a6, c7, g6"; 4th: "lazy WQ, f2 only
   for a try"). Same lesson as WCCT 10.
4. **Names again**: Nietvelt defences, B2 self-blocks, Pickabish, Stocchi blocks, Barulin, Mackenzie, Theme A Grimshaws, Levmann-like
   interferences. Our judges described the mechanisms without these names.
