## What the three together say
1. **On ordinary informal-tourney content we rank close to real judges, and we find the winner.**
2. **We count variations; they count phases and the changes across them.** "Thin: two variations" sank MK2-14
   (3rd Prize), MSK-16 (top published), MSK-22 and MSK-12 for us. For the judges, a 2x2 change across tries and key, a
   doubled Le Grand on one defence, or Mäkihovi + Zagoruiko IS the content, with no extra variations needed.
3. **Our fatal list is stricter than theirs once the phase complex is rich.** Diagram flights, tries refuted by the king,
   capturing keys (MSK-27, MSK-16, D46 in 2003), and even a double threat (MSK-13) were tolerated. What they did name:
   a double-threat try spoiling the harmony of the phases (MK2-14), a capturing try (Robert 2003), a dual in the solution.
4. **Theme vocabulary decides originality.** The judges name Mäkihovi, Burmistrov, Chepizhny, Hannelius, Banny,
   Sushkov, Barnes, Rukhlis, the Vladimirov effect, pseudo-Salazar. Our judges described the mechanics but did not name
   them, so they could not weigh what is new. A tested name in `themes` beats a description.
5. **Read every try against every defence.** MSK-12 lost half its content in our report because the judge listed only one of
   its two changing tries. Lay out a phase table (set / each try / key) x (each thematic defence) before scoring.
6. **The judge's taste matters, and a field of prize-winners compresses differences** (the published section: 9-14 points,
   correlation near zero).
7. **2004 (judged with the calibrated checklist) agreed better than 2003**: the judges see the same content and the same
   flaws as the real judge, problem after problem. What is left is weighting (refutation quality first: no rude refutations),
   generosity (place fewer), and names (Somov, Albino, Mäkihovi, Burmistrov ...: ask E. Bourd for the ones we lack).
8. **Judges have schools** (1999-2002, Morice): line strategy (bivalence, masked lines, anti-Lewmann, Franco-Latvian,
   equipollent moves) against phase counting. Read both kinds of content; the remaining disagreements are about which one a judge pays for.
9. **White correction and thematic multiple threats** (The Problemist): the strongest field showed the two biggest blind
   spots: a tertiary threat correction (1st Prize) read as "mechanical tries with a shared refutation", and English
   Novotny / Fleck read as fatal double threats. Next experiments should give the judges the published solution and theme
   (no author, no source), as a real judge has.
10. **Seven experiments, one pattern** (StrateGems 2015): with the solution in hand, our judges find the real judge's top problems
   (his 1st Prize in our top 3 in four of the seven tourneys) and agree on content almost everywhere. What remains is weighting,
   and the weighting differs from judge to judge: Robert punished capturing tries, Mladenovic rude refutations, Morice paid for
   line strategy, Gockel forgave rude moves in a threat correction. A single fixed filter overfits to the last judge read.
11. **Two regimes** (eight experiments): on complete fields with a short award (Problemesis), AUC 0.80-0.86 and the winner in our
   top 3; on strong yacpdb fields with long awards (The Problemist, StrateGems), AUC 0.66-0.70 whether or not the judges have
   the solutions. Inside a strong field the order is largely the judge's taste; our remaining fixable misses are mechanisms
   read as "mixed" (half-battery, role exchange) and named themes (Somov, Albino, Franco-Latvian...).
12. **Ten experiments** (Problemist 2011 added): complete short awards 0.80-0.86; strong long awards 0.64-0.76. Recurrent fixable
    misses: tasks and named schemes scored as "mechanical" (placed by Morice, Kopyl and the Problemist judges), line mechanisms
    read as mixed play, and missing theme names.

## Sources for scored fields (wfcc.ch, allowed in the environment on 12 Oct 2026)
`wfcc_pdf_links.txt` lists the 661 PDFs found by crawling https://www.wfcc.ch/competitions/composing/ . For #2 (section A):
WCCT 10 entries A + final award, WCCT 11 section A entries + A report (scores) + booklet, WCCT 12 section A entries
(no award yet), WCCT 7-9 books, WCCI 5-9 and 2019-21 / 2022-24 section A results and winners. WCCT entries books are
anonymous by design (country codes), so they make blind fields without an extractor; the scores live in the reports.
Download them into /tmp/claude-0/sealed/ and do not open the score files before our ranking is committed.
13. **World level inverts our weighting** (WCCI 8 A): the judges pay for content and ignore the flaws we count; our judges reward
    cleanliness. "Tolerance grows with content" must be applied at full strength for rich multi-phase problems.
14. **A full world-level field agrees with us like a judging country** (WCCT 11 A, 79 scored entries): Spearman 0.63 against the
    total, 0.47-0.56 against each country, while the countries agree among themselves at 0.34-0.72. WCCI 8 (-0.32) was a
    compressed field of winners only. The fixable misses are the same as before: a double threat paid for by a record content,
    and new task arrangements scored as "mechanical". The unfixable one without a search: anticipation.
15. **The calibration held and the next misses are about reading** (WCCT 10 A, 93 entries): Spearman 0.73, near the countries'
    agreement with each other (0.62-0.78). No double-threat or task misses this time. New ones: a theme that admits tries was
    marked down when shown "only in a try" (the 2nd-3rd place our 41st); the logical progression of a multistage try sequence
    (1st place) was read as a list of changes; white units idle after the key were noted but not punished as the countries did.
16. **Full fields measure judging; top fields measure taste** (WCCT 9 A, the 27 best): our panel 0.16 against the total, the real
    countries 0.08 against each other, and the 1st place shared (ours 1st). With WCCI 8 (winners only, -0.32) and WCCT 10/11 (all
    entries, 0.73/0.63): on a full field we judge like a country; among the best, nobody agrees except on the outstanding problem.
    Content lesson: effects the set theme itself produces (Dombrovskis, Hannelius in a threats-return theme) are the floor, not extra.
17. **A calibration can overcorrect** (WCCT 8 A, 28 entries): Spearman 0.30, inside the countries' range (mean 0.22). The WCCT 9 line
    "effects the theme produces are the floor" was applied to effects this theme does not force, and the 1st place fell to our 9th.
    Each line in the checklist needs its condition stated; it is now "what the theme's wording logically entails". Also: black
    correction must be a new error, not the avoidance of the random move's error.
