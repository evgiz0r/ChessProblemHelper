# Judging checklist, from Evgeni Bourd's sessions

| Item | Weight | Critique tag |
|---|---|---|
| Cook, second key | fatal | `cook` |
| Dual in a thematic variation | fatal | `dual` |
| Double threat | fatal in practice | solve line "threat: A/B" |
| Unprovided check in the set play | fatal | `unprovided check` |
| Promoted piece in the diagram | fatal unless unavoidable | count it yourself |
| Black king flight in the diagram | big flaw | `king escape in diagram` |
| Key by a piece out of play (edge, far, guarding nothing near the king; queens and rooks too, 8 Oct) | fatal (E. Bourd, 7 Oct) | `out-of-play key` |
| White unit in no mate of the solution and not needed for soundness | fatal | `superfluous piece` |
| White unit in no mate of the solution, there only for a try | drawback, weighed against the try's content (E. Bourd, 11 Oct; fatal on 7 Oct) | `try-only unit` |
| Officer (Q/R/B/S) whose only job is the key, in no mate after it; a pawn doing the same is cheap | usually fatal (E. Bourd, 8 Oct) | `useless key piece` |
| Same mechanism as a published problem with nothing added, or reduced | not a problem (E. Bourd, 7 Oct) | anticipation (gate: identical only) |
| Try refuted by a king move | big flaw | `king refutes try` |
| Same refutation for several thematic tries | flaw | `same refutation` |
| White unit taking part in no mate (key piece forgivable; pin-anchor king thematic) | flaw | `passive piece`, `passive king`, `cook-stopper` |
| Unit needed only for soundness | flaw | `cook-stopper` |
| Obvious Black defence with no answer | flaw | `unprovided move` |
| Defences that merely guard the threat square, mates that merely exploit the unguard | weak content | - |
| One mating move for all defences | thin | `thin play` |
| Flight-giving key, long key, quiet key by a piece with a role | plus | `flight-giving key`, `long key` |
| Unified defences / one dual-avoidance motive | plus | `dual avoidance` |
| Tries whose refutation uses the theme; changed mates between phases | plus | `thematic tries`, `Changed continuations` |
| Economy: units, pawns, airy position; aesthetics yield to content as ideas get richer | balance | `economy` |

Judgement = thematic depth, novelty and complexity against the cost in units and aesthetics. Too simple
plus big flaws is not worth it even if economical. Tries with unthematic refutations are much less
interesting. Extra (non-thematic) variations are a necessary evil unless they carry something of their own.

## Weighing, not counting (E. Bourd, session 24)

Flaws are weighed against content. A tripled theme (all four promotions of one pawn tried, each failing for
its own reason: 5K2/3P4/1N2p3/1P2k1NP/1P4P1/3p4/3B4/8) can carry three diagram flights, a flight-taking key,
king-move refutations and a king-flight variation. Clean and simple is usually judged below complex with
some flaws; the balance is partly subjective. So: name every flaw, then say what content it is buying.

### The scale: tolerance grows with content (E. Bourd, 12 Oct 2026)
"As the content grows, we need to be more tolerable to flaws. A small 1 variation problem cannot be compared to
like reciprocal changes. We pay flaws for content, and find the balance." Place the problem first, then judge:

| Content | Examples | What it can pay for |
|---|---|---|
| Light: one thematic variation or one simple paradox, often a miniature | single Dombrovskis, a Le Grand miniature | almost nothing: a try-only unit, a plug, an obvious key or a borrowed mechanism sinks it (daily No. 1) |
| Medium: several thematic variations or one change between two phases | Boros pair of changed mates, a correction with two corrections | a cook-stopper or two, a passive king, an unthematic dual after random moves |
| Rich: reciprocal changes, Zagoruiko, Le Grand + Dombrovskis together, a cycle | his Wola Gulowska 2015 5th HM (reciprocal change, 25 units), Sredba 2003-05 1st HM (Le Grand, Dombrovskis, Zagoruiko) | heavy force and pawns, several plugs (Pc4, Pb4), a unit standing on a flight (Pf6), try-only units, capturing keys and tries when thematic, unthematic duals left out of the published solution |

Never bought back at any level: a cook, a dual in a thematic variation, a double threat (the gate keeps them
fatal). Everything else is weighed: say what the problem IS, which level it reaches, then the flaws and
whether that content pays for them.

## His verdicts on the first two daily problems (7 October 2026)
- No. 1 (single Dombrovskis, 6K1/1N6/4p3/1PBk3p/8/8/4Q3/6N1): "almost plagiarised without any additional value,
  even reducing the original"; "almost a non problem with a single Dombrovskis"; the g1 knight that only makes the
  try is a big fatal flaw, "way too big of a price for such content".
- No. 2 (bishop correction, 3R4/8/6K1/4P3/3bk3/N1p3Q1/8/8): the random mechanism (line opening) is decent; the
  corrections are slightly weak, remove one; the random move almost allows the corrected mate, and the c3 pawn is a
  somewhat fake blocker of those moves; the error of the correction is not subtle enough; the main weakness, fatal,
  is the white knight out of play making the key, which makes the solution obvious.

## Symmetry and the published tries (E. Bourd, 11 Oct 2026, on 1.Sa3!)
- "by design this is a bit too symmetrical, which is a flaw in general." Two mirror-image variations with the
  same motive are less than two variations that differ.
- When several tries share a refutation, publish one try per thematic refutation, as a pair (Sc7? R6b5!,
  Sc3? R4b5!), not 2+1; keep the others in the notes.
- An edge key is acceptable when the piece has a role in the solution and its other moves make real tries: "a3
  is somewhat less obvious than the rest, making the tries more real."

## Calibration against a real award (blind experiment, Problemesis 2003, judge P. Robert; knowledge/experiments)
- A try that captures a black unit is a serious flaw (three entries refused for it), not only a capturing key.
- A named fatal flaw (a dual in a thematic phase, an idle unit in a twin) is not bought back by ambition.
- Clear and economical beats heavy and rich at the top: the 1st Prize had 7+7, no white pawns and no secondary play.
- Be stingy: an informal award places about a quarter of the entries.
- Count phases, not variations (Moscow 2016 / Moscow Ch. 2012 blind experiments): a 2x2 change across tries and key, a
  doubled Le Grand on one defence or Mäkihovi + Zagoruiko is full content; "thin: two variations" is the wrong verdict there.
- Tabulate (set / each try / key) x (each thematic defence) before scoring, so that no changing try is missed.
- Name the multi-phase theme (Mäkihovi, Burmistrov, Chepizhny, Hannelius, Banny, Rukhlis, ...) or say you cannot; real judges
  weigh originality by the name.
- Our fatal list is our composing standard. When predicting an award, a rich phase complex buys back a diagram flight,
  a king-refuted try or a capture key; a double-threat try that breaks the harmony of the phases is still noticed.
- Look at the refutations first (M. Mladenovic, Problemesis 2004 award): "rude" refutations (checks, captures, king moves to a
  flight the try gives) and flight-taking tries and keys weigh heavily with many judges. A capturing try that IS the theme
  (Albino) is accepted, and judges differ: Gockel (StrateGems 2015) placed threat-correction problems with capturing keys,
  capturing tries and open flights. Weigh rude moves against the theme; do not let them filter out a correctly shown theme.
- Read line strategy as content, not only phases (J. Morice, Problemesis 1999-2002): bivalent key/defence effects (one line
  opened while two close), masked lines and anti-Lewmann defences, Franco-Latvian, one black unit refuting all tries with moves
  equipollent to White's (theme A). A mechanical multi-phase scheme can rank below a single-phase strategic one.
- White correction (The Problemist 2014 blind experiment): when several tries of ONE white piece share a refutation, check
  whether they are the random move of a white correction; then read each specific move's error and its changed threat
  (secondary / tertiary threat correction). That structure is first-prize content, not a flaw.
- A multiple threat that the theme separates (English Novotny, Fleck, Brogi) is content; read the author's theme first.
- Changed play can be a role exchange with no common defence (a white king battery in the try, a black one in the
  solution: StrateGems 2015, 2nd Prize). An empty phase table is not the end of the reading.
